                                                                   using critical thinking to determine whether a source is reliable.

                                                                   2. Information Literacy
                                                                   The American Library Association defines information literacy
                                                                   as “a set of abilities requiring individuals to recognize when
                                                                   information is needed and have the ability to locate, evaluate,
                                                                   and use effectively the needed information.” As such,
                                                                   information literacy means that you first know how to research
                                                                   and find the sources you're looking for and how to evaluate
                                                                   whether a source is a credible and reliable source of
                                                                   information. While this may sound simple, it has become an
                                                                   increasingly difficult task with the advent of fake news,
                                                                   crowdsourced information, and anonymous internet authors.

                                                                   3. Unreliable Sources
                                                                   Journalism has changed drastically over the last few decades
                                                                   with the rise of the internet, smartphones, and social media.
                                                                   The development of 24-hour news networks has encouraged
                                                                   cable news to embrace sensationalist headlines while
                                                                   promoting the partisan viewpoints of its owners and balancing
                                                                   the needs and demands of advertisers. TV and social media
                                                                   have overtaken print as sources of news, so newspapers and
                                                                   local journalism have struggled to survive. As a result, people
                                                                   must look to other sources for their news, and oftentimes,
                                                                   these sources may have conflicts of interest that must be
                                                                   considered when evaluating whether their reporting is reliable
                                                                   and credible. There's a lot of pressure in journalism to break the
                                                                   story first, regardless of whether reporters have all the facts. As
                                                                   a result, initial reports are often wrong, and networks or
                                                                   publications may find themselves issuing corrections or
                                                                   retractions later.

                                                                   These are all important realities to keep in mind when finding
                                                                   sources on the internet. This isn't to say that all cable news is
                                                                   unreliable or that you should never trust a blog. Instead, the
                                                                   takeaway should be to do your research. When deciding
                                                                   whether or not a source is credible, ask a few key questions:

                                                                      Who wrote this text? Do they have a reputation as a credible
                                                                      scholar or expert in this field or on this topic? Is this a reliable
                                                                      organization that has demonstrated a commitment to getting

                                                                   4. Critical Thinking about Fake News
                                                                   Please read Machete and Turpin's “The Use of Critical Thinking
                                                                   to Identify Fake News: A Systematic Literature Review.” The
                                                                   article reviews the literature on how critical thinking can be
                                                                   used to identify fake news. The articles that explicitly discussed
                                                                   critical thinking found that most students do not have sufficient
                                                                   critical thinking skills to identify news that is fake.

                                                                  1. Is the information accurate, as determined by checking other
                                                                     sources?
                                                                  2. Is the article written by an authority? Is the authority
                                                                     recognized by peers?
                                                                  3. Is the information complete? Take care to note stories that
                                                                     omit key information.
                                                                  4. Is the story timely? Have there been updates or other news

                                                                  5. Was the information presented as clickbait, merely to attract
                                                                     an audience, or is the information provided as a genuine
                                                                     news story?

                                                                   Suppose in your due diligence, you answer some of the above
                                                                   questions in the negative. The question to ask then is "has this
                                                                   story been repeated elsewhere, and has it been confirmed or
                                                                   verified by reliable sources?" Any doubts on your part could
                                                                   then prevent that article from becoming part of the data for
                                                                   your sentiment analysis.

                                                                   5. Conclusion
                                                                   Clearly, creating fake news would be a violation of good ethical
                                                                   practice. As an FE, you can become more information-literate
                                                                   by learning how to assess the quality, authority,
                                                                   comprehensiveness, and timeliness of information you find on
                                                                   the web. This information may become data that you use to
                                                                   build and train your models for FE. Building information literacy
                                                                   will actually help promote good ethical practice as well.

                                                                   In concluding this module, let's remember to be honest and
                                                                   vigilant in avoiding the creation, spread, or inclusion of fake
                                                                   news in your studies. Even if you use AI to determine what is
                                                                   fake, be sure that you confirm what gets used. The saying
                                                                   "garbage in, garbage out" applies, but the situation can be
                                                                   worse if we assume fake stories to be true and make key
                                                                   decisions based on false information.
